How it’s converted

each step explains what it does
01Read Brushespending
An .abr from Photoshop 7 or later is a series of tagged sections:
  • samp holds the tip images and patt the patterns.
  • desc holds every preset's settings as an action descriptor, Photoshop's own typed key-value format.
  • Newer files add phry, the folders of the Brushes panel.
Files from Photoshop 6 and earlier are a plain list of tips, each with a size and spacing.
02Decode Tips & Patternspending
A tip image is an 8- or 16-bit grayscale coverage mask, usually compressed row by row with PackBits run-length encoding. 255 means full paint, so it becomes a Krita alpha mask as it is. Patterns are stored the same way, one colour channel at a time.
03Map Parameterspending
Settings map onto Krita's Pixel brush:
  • Each Photoshop control (pen pressure, fade, tilt, direction…) becomes a Krita sensor, and jitter becomes a Random sensor.
  • Scattering becomes Scatter. Count is imitated with tighter spacing.
  • The texture keeps its mode, since Krita 5 has Photoshop's height and burn modes. The dual brush becomes Krita's masking brush.
  • Transfer becomes Opacity and Flow sensors. Colour jitter becomes the Mix and H/S/V options.
Bristle and erodible tips, wet edges and noise have no Krita equivalent and are listed per brush.
04Write Presetspending
A .kpp preset is a PNG thumbnail with the preset XML in a text chunk. The thumbnails below are sample strokes drawn with the converted settings.
05Bundlepending
A Krita .bundle is a zip. It holds an uncompressed mimetype, a manifest with an MD5 for every file, the presets, the tips (brushes/) and the textures (patterns/). The presets are tagged with the bundle name, and with their Photoshop folder when they're in one.
